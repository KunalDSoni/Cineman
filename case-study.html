◆ National Award · Best Gujarati Film · 2017

WRONG SIDERAJU

A story of justice, class, and what it means to be human — told through a single road accident that changes everything.

DirectorAbhishek Jain
Year2016
CategoryFeature Film
RecognitionNational Award
The Insight

A collision of worlds,
not just vehicles.

Wrong Side Raju was not designed to be a message film. It was designed to be an honest one. The story begins with an accident — a wealthy car, a motorcycle, a moment of impact — and then asks a deeper question: in a country where class determines consequence, what does justice actually look like?

The insight that drove every frame: Gujarati audiences were ready to see themselves honestly. Wrong Side Raju was the deliberate choice to go further, to push the mirror closer, and to trust that the audience would not look away.

"We wanted to make a film where both characters are right and both are wrong. That moral ambiguity is where real life lives."
Visual Language

Tungsten light.
Real locations.
Unguarded faces.

The visual philosophy was built around intimacy. The camera was never supposed to be invisible — it was supposed to feel like a witness. Handheld, close, slightly uncomfortable. The cinematography leaned into practical light: the harsh afternoon sun of Ahmedabad, sodium street lamps, the blue-white of hospital corridors.

Color grading pulled toward the warm desaturation of real memory — not the heightened tones of escapist cinema, but the lived-in palette of a city that doesn't dress up for cameras.

Impact

What the film moved.

Wrong Side Raju won the 64th National Film Award for Best Gujarati Film. The recognition that mattered most was quieter: the conversations the film sparked in homes, college debates, and newspaper columns about class, privilege, and the machinery of justice.
