WRONG SIDERAJU

A story of justice, class, and what it means to be human — told through a single road accident that changes everything.

Director Abhishek Jain
Release Year 2016
Category Feature Film
Recognition National Award
The Insight

A collision of worlds,
not just vehicles.

Wrong Side Raju was not designed to be a message film. It was designed to be an honest one. The story begins with an accident — a wealthy car, a motorcycle, a moment of impact — and then asks a deeper question: in a country where class determines consequence, what does justice actually look like?

The insight that drove every frame: Gujarati audiences were ready to see themselves honestly. They had grown comfortable with feel-good cinema — CineMan's own earlier films included. Wrong Side Raju was the deliberate choice to go further, to push the mirror closer, and to trust that the audience would not look away.

"We wanted to make a film where both characters are right and both characters are wrong. That moral ambiguity is where real life lives."
Visual Language

Tungsten light.
Real locations.
Unguarded faces.

The visual philosophy was built around intimacy. The camera was never supposed to be invisible — it was supposed to feel like a witness. Handheld, close, slightly uncomfortable. The cinematography leaned into practical light: the harsh afternoon sun of Ahmedabad, the sodium glow of street lamps, the blue-white of hospital corridors.

Color grading pulled toward the warm desaturation of real memory — not the heightened tones of escapist cinema, but the lived-in palette of a city that doesn't dress up for cameras. Every frame was graded to feel like something you have seen before.

Production Philosophy

Cast from truth,
not from type.

The casting of Wrong Side Raju was a philosophical decision as much as a creative one. Abhishek Jain sought performers who carried the specific social weight of their characters in their bodies — not actors playing class, but actors who understood it from the inside.

Rehearsals were extensive and improvisational. The script was a structure, not a cage. Certain scenes were rewritten on location when the reality of the place revealed something the writing had not anticipated. This responsiveness — the willingness to let the world inform the story — is a signature of how CineMan approaches all its productions.

[ Behind The Scenes ]

The locations were Ahmedabad — fully, specifically, unromanticized. The city's ring roads, its courtrooms, its upper-middle-class drawing rooms and working-class chawls. The geographical specificity was part of the honesty: this story happens here, in this city, because these social dynamics are alive here.

Impact

What the film moved.

Wrong Side Raju won the 64th National Film Award for Best Gujarati Film. But the recognition that mattered most was quieter: the number of Gujarati audiences who saw themselves in both characters. The conversations the film sparked in homes, in college debates, in newspaper columns about class and privilege and the machinery of justice.
